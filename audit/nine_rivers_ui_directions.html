The interface should be made of the same things as the game

Three directions, all playable below. Each takes one real object from the game's own world and makes the whole interface out of it — rather than putting Chinese decoration on a generic mobile menu.

The problem to solve

Why the current menu reads as a template

Looking at your live screenshots: every option is a full-width rounded rectangle with a gold border and an emoji, stacked eight deep. That's the default shape of a mobile menu — and emoji icons are the single strongest tell that a UI wasn't art-directed, because they're someone else's artwork rendered in someone else's style.

The opportunity is sitting right there in the game: you just built beautiful tiles. Ivory ceramic, gold leaf, basalt, rose porcelain — with real bevels and real material. If the menu is made of those same surfaces, the interface stops being a layer on top of the game and becomes part of it. No other Mahjong game does this, because most don't have the tile art to spend.

Direction A · recommended

The Rack — every menu item is a tile

Menu rows are literal mahjong tiles lying face-up on the water: ivory ceramic face, the warm biscuit underside showing as a 3 px lip, a carved glyph where a suit symbol would be. They press like tiles — the lip compresses on tap, the same tactile language as play.

The featured row carries the gold band of a banded triple, which already means "this one is worth more" to anyone who has played a single level. That's a UI accent the player has been taught by the game.

The whole menu is a hanging scroll with turned wooden rollers and gold end-caps. Sub-screens unroll downward rather than sliding in. Quieter and more literary than A — stronger for the Calm Journey, weaker for the roguelite mode, which wants urgency.

Whichever you pick

The rules that make it read as premium

Kill every emoji

🎵 📳 👁️ 🀄 are someone else's artwork in someone else's style, and they render differently on every Android skin. Replace with carved glyphs (音 振 眼) in the game's own serif, or thin gold line icons. This one change does more than anything else on this page.

Hairlines, not borders

Every panel currently has a 1 px gold box around it. Boxes inside boxes flatten hierarchy. Use a single hairline rule that fades at both ends, and let spacing do the separating.

One seal, not many accents

Cinnabar red is the strongest colour you have. Spend it in exactly one place per screen — the seal stamp, or the confirm action. Everywhere else is jade, gold and ivory.

Stop full-width stacking

Eight identical full-width buttons give the eye nothing to grip. Vary the row height, let the primary action be visibly heavier, and put secondary items behind one entry point.

Frost over the pond, not over black

Blur the live background so the koi drift behind the panel. That is what makes a UI feel like it's floating in the world. Real blur only on full-screen modals — a pre-blurred translucent panel with fine noise reads the same for a fraction of the cost on budget Android.

Tiles fall, sheets unfold

Two motions only. Tiles settle with weight (fast in, slight overshoot, stop). Sheets unfold (translate + scale, 260 ms, no bounce). Consistency of motion is half of what "expensive" means.
